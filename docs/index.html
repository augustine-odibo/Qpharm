pharmQ plans how medicines move from manufacturing plants and import gateways → distribution centers → pharmacies and health facilities. Two regions are bundled: the United States (50 states, DC, Puerto Rico) and Africa (all 54 countries).

  1. Demand: population × per-capita prescribing rate × local variation, per SKU and zone. About 19 pharmacies per 100k residents in the US and 8 health outlets per 100k in Africa; very populous countries are split into several zones.
  2. Forecast: Holt-Winters on 3 years of weekly history; additive or multiplicative seasonality chosen by back-test.
  3. Network design: a mixed-integer program chooses DCs and assigns each zone × handling class (ambient, cold chain, controlled substances) to one DC, balancing fixed DC cost against freight. Cold goods only go to refrigerated DCs, controlled substances only to licensed-vault DCs, and island or overly long lanes go by air. In Africa, crossing a border adds a customs cost per pack and about 4 days of clearance, and roads are slower and less direct (1.45× straight-line distance, about 400 km a day).
  4. Inventory: safety stock, reorder point (average and peak season) and order quantity (EOQ capped by shelf life) per DC × SKU.
  5. Simulation: 365 days of random demand and lead times, FEFO lot expiry, optional plant or port outages with backup-source failover.
  6. Risk: 0–100 shortage score per SKU from sourcing, service gap, volatility, lead time, handling and shelf life.

Populations: US 2020 Census; Africa 2024 UN World Population Prospects estimates. Costs, capacities, per-capita use and lead times are illustrative placeholders.
